**Poetry by Walusimbi Leon**

### First Rain

It begins not with sound
but with a smell—
that first shudder of dust
turning to earth,
the way a held breath
finally remembers
it was meant to be let go.

I stood at the window
of the room I no longer live in,
and the ground outside
was the color of old coins,
and I thought:
even the dirt has been waiting.

The first drops come down
like misremembered names,
each one a small erasure
on the pavement's dry tongue.
The air goes amber,
then green,
then that particular gray
of a bruise that is healing.

Someone is singing
in a language I almost know.
The song is about a river
that carried a letter
to the wrong village.
The song is about me.

I press my palm to the glass.
The glass is cool.
The glass is learning
what cold means
from the other side.

### What the Roof Knows

The roof knows the rain
the way the body knows grief—
by weight, by rhythm,
by the long patience
of being beaten
into something new.

All night it practices
the same word:
*again, again, again,*
until the word loses meaning
and becomes weather,
becomes the only verb.

I lie beneath it
counting the syllables
of water.
I am learning
to conjugate absence:
I missed, you missed,
it will keep missing.

The gutter overflows.
The garden floods.
Somewhere a child
has left a red bucket
out to fill,
and the rain
is incredibly gentle
with that small
and patient mouth.

### Letter Never Sent

I write you from the season
between two rains,
where the air is a held note
and the clouds are deciding
whether to stay.

I have been thinking
about the word *return*,
how it contains the word *turn*,
how everything that comes back
must first show its back.

You would like this weather—
the way the light goes through
one more change
before committing.
The way the leaves
turn their silver undersides
up, like hands
offering what they cannot hold.

I have been keeping
a list of small disasters:
the spider plant that died
of too much water,
the train I missed
by one breath,
the dream in which you
were wearing a coat
the color of this sky.

I don't know what to do
with the list.
I don't know what to do
with the word *return*.

I fold the letter
into the shape of a bird.
I unfold it.
I fold it again—
this time into nothing,
into the shape of rain
falling on rain,
into the shape
of a thing
that was never sent
and still arrived.

### The Grammar of Water

Rain has its own syntax.
The subject is always *the ground*.
The verb is always *becoming*.
The object is whatever
is left open.

I am learning
to read the weather
the way you read a face—
for the small betrayals,
the almost-breaks,
the moment the eye
goes elsewhere
and the storm
follows.

Every drop is a subordinate clause
modifying the storm.
Every storm is a paragraph
about leaving.
I have been the footnote
to your longest rain.

Tonight I watch
the water walk
across the window,
each bead a sentence
looking for its period.
The glass is the page.
The page is the sky.

I want to tell you
that language is just
weather we can hear.

I want to tell you
that I have stopped waiting
for the thunder
that follows the light.

### Small Weathers

The kettle whistles—
a small storm
in the kitchen.
The steam rises
like a question
answered by the ceiling.

The fridge hums
in the key of constant.
The clock ticks
in the key of going.
The rain outside
keeps its own time,
and I am beginning
to prefer it.

I have made tea
and forgotten
to drink it.
The cup cools.
The cup learns
the temperature
of the room I am in,
which is the temperature
of the room
I am always in.

The plant in the corner
has turned
toward the window
like a rumor
traveling toward truth.

Everything in this house
is practicing patience.
Everything in this house
is learning
to be rained on
without moving.

### What the Clouds Carry

The clouds are carrying
water from the ocean
to the inland fields,
practicing the oldest trade—
theft by evaporation,
delivery by gravity.

I have watched them
all my life
and never once
asked what they carry
that isn't water:
the letters
I didn't write,
the names
I didn't say,
the last word
of every conversation
that ended
too early.

The clouds are carrying
your voice,
I am certain.
They have been carrying it
for years,
through drought,
through everything,
and tonight it arrives
as the smallest rain,
a mist so fine
it is almost a thought.

I stand in it.
I do not move.
I let it settle
on my shoulders,
my open hands,
my face
that has been waiting
for this weather
all its life.

### The Rain's Vocabulary

The rain has a word
for *almost*—
it uses it
when it pauses,
when it holds one drop
suspended
between decision and descent,
the way you held
the door
that night
in the city,
the way you said
my name
and did not say
the rest.

The rain has a word
for *memory*—
it is the sound
of water
finding an old path,
a groove in the stone
where water has gone
for a thousand years.
I am that groove.
I am the memory
of water.

The rain has a word
for *you*.
It is the quietest word.
It is the sound
rain makes
on a roof
when the house
is empty.
It is the sound
rain makes
on a roof
when the house
is full
of someone
waiting.

I am learning
the rain's vocabulary.
I am learning
to say *you*
without moving
my lips.

### After the Rain

The air is a rinsed glass.
The ground is a dark mirror.
The birds come back
to the trees
like punctuation
arriving
at the end
of a long sentence.

I walk the wet path
through the village,
and my boots
make small sounds
that the earth
is quick to forgive.
The leaves have been
translated.
Everything shines
with the effort
of being seen.

In the market,
the fruit has a new skin.
The old women
are shaking out
their umbrellas,
and the sound
is like applause
for something
that happened
in their absence.

I think of you
in the city,
how the rain there
is different—
it falls on glass,
on concrete,
on the umbrellas
of strangers
who will never
know each other's names.

Here, the rain
falls on the same ground
my father walked,
and his father,
and the cattle,
and the seeds.

Here, the rain
is a witness.
Here, I am
a witness
to the rain.

### The Weight of Rain

I have been thinking
about the weight of rain—
how a single drop
is almost nothing,
how a storm
is almost everything,
how the difference
is only
a matter of time.

I have been carrying
this weather
inside me
for so long
I forget
it is weather.
I think it is
my own weight.
I think it is
what I am.

But tonight
I set it down.
I stand in the rain
with my arms open
and let the water
tell me
what I am:
not the storm,
not the silence
after the storm,
but the small window
in which rain
is visible.

I am the window.
I am the looking.
I am the rain
and the one
who is rained on.

I am the weight,
and I am the rain,
and I am the ground
that receives it,
and I am the seed
that drinks it,
and I am the one
who will remember
this weather
long after
the weather
has gone.

### What the Rain Remembers (I)

The rain remembers
the first roof it ever fell on—
a thatch of elephant grass,
a village with no name
except the one
the ancestors gave it
by living there.

The rain remembers
the first hand
that caught it—
a child's palm,
open like a question,
waiting to be answered.

That child was my mother.
That child was my father's mother.
That child was everyone
who ever stood
in the open
and believed
the sky
was speaking
to them
alone.

The rain remembers
every roof since.
Every hand.
Every open mouth.
Every closed door.
Every open door.

I want to believe
the rain remembers
the exact shape
of your hand
the last time
I saw it—
the way it lifted
in a wave
or a warning,
the way it vanished
into the weather
that was always
between us.

The rain remembers.
The rain keeps.
The rain is the long memory
of everything
that could not stay.

---

### What the Rain Remembers (II)

In the city,
the rain remembers
a different kind of falling.
It remembers
the slick of the taxi lights,
the neon bleeding
into puddles,
the crowds
that parted
and did not meet again.

It remembers
the night I walked
from your building
to the station,
counting the minutes
between streetlights,
feeling the weight
of what I had said
and what I had not said,
feeling the rain
translate me
into the language
of the almost.

The rain fell on us
differently.
On you,
it was weather—
something to be survived,
avoided,
forgotten.
On me,
it was scripture—
something to be studied,
repeated,
kept.

We stood in the same rain
and were rained on
by different storms.

I have been learning
to live with this:
the rain remembers
what we could not.

### Litany for the Dry Season

For the months
without water,
for the cracked earth,
for the prayer
that is just
a word
for thirst: *rain*.

For the well
that gives
only dust,
for the cattle
that low
in a language
older than any god,
for the children
who know
the sound
of rain
on a distant hill
before it arrives: *rain*.

For the grandmother
who reads the sky
like a letter
from a son
who never writes,
for the way she says
*it will come*
and the village
believes her:
*rain*.

For the first cloud,
for the first smell,
for the first drop
that is not yet
rain
but is the promise of rain,
for the promise
that is its own
kind of water:
*rain*.

For what comes
after the rain,
for what grows
in the held breath,
for the green
that is the world
remembering
its color:
*rain*.

For you,
who are not here,
who are the drought
and the rain,
who are the word
I say
when the sky
finally opens:
*rain*.

### The Last Rain

It will come
the way all rain comes—
without asking,
without warning,
without knowing
it is the last.

It will fall
on the same roof,
the same ground,
the same hands
that have known
every rain before it.
It will be ordinary.
It will be everything.

I will stand
in the same window
where I stood
the first time,
and I will smell
the dust
turning to earth,
and I will remember
that this
is what I was
always waiting for.

The rain will not know
it is the last.
The rain will not know
it is the first.

The rain will simply fall,
the way I have loved you—
without knowing
what it was,
without knowing
what it meant,
without knowing
it was the last time
the sky would open
in this way,
at this hour,
over this field,
over this town,
over this heart.

And I will receive it
the way the ground receives—
without holding,
without asking,
without keeping.

I will be rained on.
I will be rain.
I will be the memory
of rain
falling on a world
that was briefly
and completely
wet.

### The Weight of Rain (II)

The weight of rain
is not the weight
of water.
It is the weight
of what the water
carries:
the dust of a thousand
first rains,
the salt of a thousand
last rains,
the names
that were said
in the rain,
the names
that were not.

The weight of rain
is the weight
of attention—
the way the world
pauses
when the first drop lands,
the way the pavement
holds its breath,
the way the leaf
turns its face
upward
to receive.

I have been rained on
in two countries.
I have been rained on
in love
and in the absence
of love.
I have been rained on
the way a field
is rained on—
without choice,
without question,
with the simple
and total
surrender
of one who knows
the rain
is the only
gift
the sky
has ever known
how to give.

And I have learned
to carry
this rain
the way the clouds carry it—
not as weight,
but as purpose,
not as burden,
but as the slow
and certain
gathering
of everything
that must eventually
fall.

### Postscript: The First Rain, Again

It begins the same way.
The smell.
The pause.
The small
and singular
knowledge
that the earth
was thirsty
and did not know
how to say it.

The rain comes
the way a name comes
when you have been
trying all day
to remember it—
suddenly,
completely,
as if it had never
been forgotten.

I am standing
in the same place.
I am smelling
the same dust.
I am feeling
the same first drop
on the same
open hand.

The rain does not know
this is the first time.
The rain does not know
it has rained before.
The rain only knows
the falling,
the landing,
the becoming.

I am learning
to be like the rain.
I am learning
to fall
without remembering
the fall.
I am learning
to arrive
without knowing
I have arrived.

This is the first rain.
This is every rain.
This is the weight
I have been carrying
and the weight
that carries me.

This is the rain.
This is the rain.
This is the rain.
